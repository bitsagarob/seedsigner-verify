    3 Put it on the card
    

  
    You are on a phone. This step needs a computer.
     Everything up to here is done, so pick it up on a laptop when you can.
    

  
    Use Raspberry Pi Imager. It is free, and it checks the card afterwards.

    Write it without a GUI tool
    
    
    Raspberry Pi Imager is open source, Apache licensed, and worth recommending. It also reports
       usage to rpi-imager-stats.raspberrypi.com: the version, your
       operating system, CPU architecture and locale, plus which image you picked when it comes from
       their own repository. Choosing your own file is not that, but if you would rather run nothing
       extra at all, dd is already on your machine.
    
    
    
    
    Get the device wrong and you overwrite a disk with no confirmation and no undo. Check it twice.
    

    
    Or keep Imager and switch the telemetry off
    
    
    

    
    Verify the card, not just the file
    
    
    Writing can go wrong after a correct file. Imager verifies by default; confirm it did. On the
       smartcard firmware the device itself can verify a freshly written card under MicroSD Card Tools.
    
    
    
  
    

    



    
  
    4 Start it up
    

  
    Put the card in and plug in the power.
    
  
    Insert the card, power it. First boot takes roughly twenty seconds.
    

  
    The device does not check the software again when it starts, so it
     runs whatever is on the card and the check you did in step 2 is the only one there will ever be.
     Keep the card with the device.
    
  
    The device performs no signature check at boot, so it executes
     whatever the card holds and step 2 is the only verification in the chain. Keep the card with
     the device, and treat a card you did not write yourself as untrusted.
    

  
    
    
    Your screen needs one setting changed
    
    
    Your model has the bigger screen, so the picture looks wrong at first.
       Fix it once and it stays fixed.
    
    
    The 320x240 panel is not the default. Set it once, with persistence on, or you
       will redo it every boot.
    
    
      
      
    1. Go to Settings.
      2. 
      
    2. Turn on Persistent settings first. Without this, the next step is forgotten every time you switch off.
      3. 
      
    3. Then Advanced, then Hardware, then Display type.
      4. 
      
    4. Choose st7789 320x240.
      5. 
    
    
    
    
      Skip the menus: put a small file on the card instead
      
    Before you eject the card, save a file called settings.json in the top level of
         the card containing exactly this. It sets the same two options at first boot.
    
      
    
      
    Type it yourself rather than taking ours. It is two lines and it means you are not trusting
         a file from your seller on the device that will hold your keys.

    The honest ceiling
    
    
    Software cannot verify hardware. Reproducible builds push the boundary a long way but they do
       not remove it, and no page can prove itself. Everything here is designed so you can check it
       with tools you did not get from us. That is the most this can honestly offer.

    5 Check the hardware
    

  
    Open the case and hold your boards next to these pictures. Look at the
     parts and the lines between them, not at the printed text.
    
  
    Reference images for each board in the device. Compare part placement,
     part count and traces. Markings and date codes vary by batch. The layout must not.

    Pinch or scroll to zoom, drag to move. Tap a number to go to that part.
    

  
      

  
      These are pictures of one board each, not of yours. The text on the
     chips, the date codes and the colours can be different. The place, the shape and the number of
     the parts, and the lines between them, must be the same.
      
  
      One reference board each, not your unit. Chip markings, date codes,
     solder mask shade and second-source passives can differ. Placement, footprints and traces must
     match. A visual check finds added parts, extra wires and a swapped board. It cannot see inside a
     chip or inside the inner layers of a board.

       hole coordinates used for the warp, so you can check where every pixel came from.
      
    
      What looking cannot prove
      
    
      A chip with the right shape and the right text can still be a different chip. The Pi Zero
       has copper layers inside the board that nobody sees without an X-ray. A good match lowers
       the risk of a crude implant or a swapped board. It does not prove the hardware is honest.
